Instability at two gateways to global energy, what Bangladesh needs to do

Geopolitical tensions surrounding the Straits of Hormuz and Bab al-Mandeb have brought the stability of global energy supplies into renewed question. A prolonged standoff on these vital waterways could lead to increased oil prices and transportation costs, supply disruptions, and additional pressure on import-dependent economies. For Bangladesh, this crisis is not just about the threat of rising energy import costs; it is also deeply related to foreign exchange reserves, industrial production, and long-term development plans. On the other hand,
Saudi Arabia's alternative oil transportation system and geopolitical flexibility provide important examples of crisis management. In this reality, exploring Bangladesh's own energy resources, reducing import dependence, investing in renewable energy, and strengthening strategic cooperation with Saudi Arabia may be effective ways to ensure future energy security. After the oil embargo of the 1970s, the international energy system is once again facing a major test.
